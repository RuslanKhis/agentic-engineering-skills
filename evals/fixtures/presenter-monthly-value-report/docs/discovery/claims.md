# Discovery brief: claims triage at Fenwick Mutual

Status: accepted 2026-07-29 by Helen Okafor (Head of Claims Operations)
Prepared by: Larkspur Digital

## 1. The journey today

A first notification of loss arrives by web form or email. A triage handler reads it, opens
the policy in the policy administration system, decides the claim type and urgency, notes the
cover position and routes the claim to a team. 9 % of claims are re-routed after triage because
the first routing was wrong.

## 2. Where it breaks

- Handlers re-read long policy wordings to find the clause that applies.
- Urgent claims (escape of water, uninhabitable home) wait in the same queue as routine ones.
- Notes are inconsistent, so the receiving team re-checks cover.

## 3. First workflow

Triage of new home claims (motor follows only if home works).

## 4. Outcome, baseline and counter-metric

- **Outcome measure:** median triage time per claim, from the claims MI report, monthly, owned
  by the claims MI analyst.
- **Baseline:** 21 minutes (August 2026 before the pilot, 400 claims, claims MI report); covers
  new home claims only.
- **Target:** 12 minutes or less on pilot claims.
- **Counter-metric:** re-routing rate after triage; baseline 9 % (same sample).
- **Threshold sentences:**
  - If the median triage time on pilot claims is above 15 minutes after four pilot weeks, we
    will stop the pilot and review the drafts with the team leads.
  - If the re-routing rate rises above 9 % in any month, we will pause the assistant's urgency
    field until the cause is found.
- **How the measure reaches a KPI line:** handler hours per claim in the claims operations
  cost line.

## 5. Technology fit

An agent with tools (policy lookup) drafting a recommendation for a person to accept; the
decision stays with the handler.

## 6. Unit economics and people

- Volume: about 1,200 new home claims a month in the pilot queue.
- Accepted cost ceiling: £900 a month for the pilot (model, hosting and database).
- 14 triage handlers in Leeds; two team leads.
