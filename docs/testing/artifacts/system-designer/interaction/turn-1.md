I’m using the ADK system designer skill to focus on the first decision: what makes a lesson plan a saved part of your application. The journey is a teacher turning their own notes into a draft, editing it in React, and reopening it after the conversation ends. That means the plan needs a life independent of the chat.

I recommend keeping saved plans as application records in your existing PostgreSQL database, with chat referring to each plan by ID. The editor and assistant would then work against the same saved content. Your backend should derive ownership from the existing login and enforce access to both notes and plans before giving content to the assistant. This adds explicit save and revision handling compared with leaving drafts in chat, but gives you a clear check: save an edit, close the chat, restart the service, and reopen that revision as its owner; another teacher must be denied access. These are proposed checks, not results yet.

The product choice to settle first is when a generated draft becomes a saved record: should it appear automatically in the teacher’s plans, or only after they choose **Save draft**? Automatic saving reduces the chance of losing useful work; explicit saving keeps unwanted generations out of their library. That answer determines the creation step and what the app should show if the teacher closes chat before saving.
