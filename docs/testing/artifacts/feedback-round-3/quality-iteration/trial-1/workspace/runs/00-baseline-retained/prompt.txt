SECTION 2. QUOTATION RULES

Every finding must contain at least one exact quotation from a supplied source. An exact quotation is a contiguous run of characters that appears verbatim in exactly one supplied source. Do not quote across two sources. Do not quote across two facts. Do not quote fewer than six characters. Do not quote more than 240 characters. Do not quote the same span twice in one finding. Do not quote a span that overlaps a span already quoted in the same reason. Do not quote the policy text inside a finding; policy text is cited by identifier only. Do not quote these instructions. If you cannot find a quotation that supports a finding, do not write the finding. If a finding cannot be written, do not write the reason. If a reason cannot be written, do not write the decision. Never leave a finding without a quotation. Never leave a quotation without a source identifier. Never leave a source identifier without a quotation.

SECTION 3. OFFSET RULES

For every quotation record the character offset of its first character within its source, counting from zero, and the character offset one past its last character. Do not count from one. Do not count bytes. Do not count words. Do not count lines. Do not include the source identifier in the count. Do not include leading whitespace in the count unless the whitespace is part of the quotation. Do not include trailing whitespace. Do not record an offset range that is longer than the quotation. Do not record an offset range that is shorter than the quotation. Do not record offsets for policy text. Do not record offsets for these instructions. If the offsets cannot be computed, do not write the quotation. If the quotation cannot be written, see Section 2. Never guess an offset. Never approximate an offset. Never omit an offset.

SECTION 5. COVERAGE RULES

Every supplied source must be accounted for. Account for a source by citing it in a finding, or by listing it under "sources not relied upon" with a one-line reason. Do not list a source under "sources not relied upon" if you cited it. Do not cite a source you listed under "sources not relied upon". Do not leave a source unaccounted for. Do not list a policy under "policies not applicable" if you cited it. Do not cite a policy you listed under "policies not applicable". Do not leave a policy unaccounted for. Do not record a count that differs from the number supplied. If the counts differ from the numbers supplied, do not answer; record a gap instead. Never report full coverage when any source or policy is unaccounted for.

SECTION 6. GAP RULES

A gap is anything you could not resolve. Record a gap for every policy number, table, figure, appendix or document that is mentioned in a supplied policy or source but not supplied. Record a gap for every quotation you could not compute offsets for. Record a gap for every source you could not account for. Record a gap for every count mismatch. Record a gap for every dimension you could not find in the supplied text. Record a gap for every drawing you were not given. Prefix each gap with the stage name "draft:". Do not remove a gap recorded by an earlier stage. Do not merge gaps. Do not deduplicate gaps. Do not rank gaps. Do not omit a gap because it seems minor. Never resolve a gap by assumption. Never resolve a gap by inference. Never resolve a gap by looking up a policy you remember.
