# Case studies for ADK projects and Google's co-marketing channels

Primary-source review, 15 October 2026. Every page below was read on that
date unless stated. Google's programs, portals and brand rules change; the
partner portal and Partner Marketing Studio are behind a login, so what they
contain is reported here only from Google's public descriptions of them.

## Conclusion

A company that has built an ADK agent on Google Cloud may already say so in
plain text, using Google's approved attribution phrases, without asking anyone.
Everything beyond that (Google Cloud logo, a joint announcement, a story on
cloud.google.com/customers, a Next session, Marketplace distribution) runs
through a named Google contact: the account team for customers, the partner
manager and Partner Marketing Studio for Partner Advantage members. There is
no public self-service form for "feature my customer story". The published
Google stories that exist share one shape (results box of three or four
quantified bullets, challenge, solution with named products, customer quotes
with job titles, an industry/location/products footer), and they rarely state
a baseline or measurement method. The skill should therefore draft a stronger
artefact than Google typically publishes (numbers with method and timeframe),
use only approved attribution language, and produce a submission pack the
account team can act on.

## What the sources actually establish

### 1. Customers may say they use Google Cloud; everything else needs consent

The Google Cloud Platform Terms, section 9 (Publicity): "Customer may state
publicly that it is a Google Cloud customer and display Brand Features in
accordance with the Branding Guidelines", and "Google may use Customer's name
and Brand Features in online or offline promotional materials of the
Services". Neither party may otherwise use the other's Brand Features or issue
press releases, blog posts, speeches, social media posts or investor
announcements about the Services or the Agreement without the other's written
consent. [Google Cloud Terms](https://cloud.google.com/terms), read 15 Oct 2026.
Enterprise customers on a negotiated agreement may have different terms; check.

The Branding Guidelines the terms link to are a PDF, "Customer Co-branding
Guidelines, Updated April 2026, Audience: All customers". It is the single
most useful document found. Documented there: any customer may, without
approval, make "a plain text mention of our Google Cloud brand name and product
name(s), link to our content, quote publicly available writings, and feature
badges you've qualified for" in website copy, blog posts, ebooks, social posts
and collateral (not in-product). Rules: never shorten to "GCP" or "GC"; do not
say "Google" alone when the technology was Google Cloud; never translate the
name; set the context that a product is from Google Cloud; do not imply a
formal partnership when only a supply agreement exists. Approved attribution
language: "Built on Google Cloud" or "Developed on Google Cloud" (customer used
Google Cloud products, no implementation guidance from Google); "Built with
Google Cloud" (multiple products, some Google guidance); "Built with AI from
Google Cloud" (multiple AI products, not solely Gemini); "Built with Google
Gemini" (Gemini model via Vertex AI, AI Studio or open source). Never "Powered
by" (reserved for true co-engineering and needs Brand & Creative approval), and
never "with Google Cloud AI". Never approved: "A partner of Google Cloud",
"Acme is powered by Google Cloud", "We are preferred by Google Cloud", "the
Acme Google Cloud solution", or combining the Google Cloud name or logo with
your own. Logo use requires a request submitted by "your Google Cloud contact"
to the Cloud Brand team; Google parent-brand use goes via Google Brand Studio.
Partner Network members are told to use the partner brand guidelines in
Partner Marketing Studio instead.
[Co-branding PDF](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/external_customer_co_branding_eligibility.pdf),
read 15 Oct 2026.

**Apply here:** the skill should hard-code the approved phrases and the banned
ones, default the draft to "Built with Google Cloud" or "Built with Google
Gemini" according to what was actually used, say "Google Cloud Agent
Development Kit (ADK)" on first mention, keep logos out of the draft, and list
"logo and joint-announcement approval via Google Cloud contact" as an open
item rather than something the agent can resolve.

### 2. Google's general brand rules add the trademark mechanics

Google's Brand Resource Center allows referring to Google products "in an
informational context in plain text" and prohibits implying endorsement,
incorporating Google marks into product or company names, and imitating
Google's visual identity. Partnerships guidance: logos are for formal partners;
"featured companies" and similar limited relationships "may be allowed to
reference Google in plain text without using our logo, as long as such
reference is accurate", otherwise "ask your Google contact to reach out to our
brand team on your behalf". The trademark rules say to use a mark as an
adjective with a generic noun, spell it exactly as listed, and never
hyphenate, abbreviate or combine it. The trademark list includes Google Cloud,
Gemini and Vertex AI; ADK is not listed.
[Guidance](https://about.google/brand-resource-center/guidance/),
[Partnerships](https://about.google/brand-resource-center/guidance/partnerships),
[Trademark rules](https://partnermarketinghub.withgoogle.com/brands/google/trademarks-and-terms/trademark-guidelines-for-proper-usage/),
[Trademark list](https://about.google/brand-resource-center/trademark-list/),
all read 15 Oct 2026.

**Apply here:** a lint pass on the draft: product names spelled as on the list,
no "GCP", no possessives or plurals of marks, no Google logo in the file, no
company name containing a Google mark.

### 3. How a Google Cloud customer story is made is not publicly documented

cloud.google.com/customers is a filterable gallery (product category, industry,
region) with a "Contact sales" button and no "share your story" form. Google
employs customer reference marketing managers (a job listing describes
onboarding reference customers and placing them in blogs, videos and talks),
but the program has no public application page; the listing is secondary
evidence of an internal program, not a documented process.
[Customer stories](https://cloud.google.com/customers), read 15 Oct 2026;
[job listing summary](https://powertofly.com/jobs/detail/1819608) (page now
returns 410; text seen only in search excerpts, 15 Oct 2026).

**Apply here:** the skill's "submit" step is: send the pack to the Google Cloud
account team (or the Google for Startups contact if enrolled) and ask them to
route it to customer reference marketing and the Cloud Brand team. State
plainly that it is an ask, not a form.

### 4. Partner Advantage: co-marketing exists, but behind the partner login

Google's public partner site describes Select, Premier and Diamond tiers and
paths for co-selling, services and technology, with "Automatic tracking" of
successful projects, but lists no marketing benefits publicly. A Google blog
post (5 Feb 2025) states that Partner Marketing Studio is available to
"Partner and Premier level" members and provides a content library, brand
guidelines and logos, campaign templates, co-branded content generation with
Gemini, "live support from Google Cloud marketing experts", "access to Google
Cloud speakers for events" and a network of vetted agencies.
[Partner Network](https://partners.cloud.google.com/),
[Partner Marketing Studio post](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/partners/google-clouds-partner-marketing-studio-now-features-gemini),
read 15 Oct 2026.

**Apply here:** if the company is a Partner Advantage member, the pack goes to
the partner manager and the partner brand guidelines in the Studio override the
customer PDF. The skill should ask which relationship applies before choosing
attribution language.

### 5. Startup, GDE and Innovators programs are not case-study channels

The Google for Startups Cloud Program documents credits, training and expert
access by tier; its site has a "Success stories" section but no public
submission route. GDE requires referral by a Googler or an active GDE and is
for individuals. Innovators is a free developer community with recognition for
blogs and talks.
[Startup benefits](https://cloud.google.com/startup/benefits),
[GDE FAQ](https://developers.google.com/community/experts/faq),
[Innovators](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/training-certifications/get-to-know-the-google-cloud-innovators-program),
read 15 Oct 2026.

**Apply here:** mention these only as "who your Google contact might be"
(startup program manager) or as individual-profile side effects of publishing.

### 6. Distribution channels: Marketplace yes, Agent Garden no

Listing on Cloud Marketplace requires Partner Network membership, a vendor
agreement, a production-ready product hosted primarily on Google Cloud, the
Project Info Form, and for an "AI agent as a service" an A2A Agent Card that
Google validates, plus product-details, pricing and technical-integration
reviews before the listing goes public. Agent Garden is described as a curated
library of Google-provided samples with no stated contribution path, so treat
"Google only" as the working assumption.
[Marketplace requirements](https://docs.cloud.google.com/marketplace/docs/partners/get-started),
[Agent Card](https://docs.cloud.google.com/marketplace/docs/partners/ai-agents/agent-card),
[Publish](https://docs.cloud.google.com/marketplace/docs/partners/ai-agents/publish),
[Agent Garden](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/build/agent-garden),
read 15 Oct 2026.

**Apply here:** Marketplace is a product-distribution decision, not part of the
case-study flow; the skill should only note it as "what's next" when the agent
is a sellable product.

### 7. ADK community showcase paths are open and lightweight

The ADK contributing guide lists seven repositories and requires a Google CLA.
`google/adk-samples` accepts community recipes under `contrib/` through pull
request review, with automated checks. The community page lists tutorials,
courses and translations, points to an "ADK Agent Directory" gallery of
"production-ready ADK agents", and disclaims Google support for external
resources. Google Cloud's Medium publication adds writers on request in the
community forum, with Medium's publication review afterwards.
[Contributing guide](https://adk.dev/community/contributing-guide/),
[adk-samples CONTRIBUTING](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/adk-samples/main/CONTRIBUTING.md),
[Community](https://adk.dev/community/),
[Medium thread](https://discuss.google.dev/t/publishing-blogs-on-medium-community-page/271781),
read 15 Oct 2026.

**Apply here:** these reuse the technical half of the case study (a sanitised
sample under `contrib/`, a Medium post). Submission needs a CLA and a
scrubbed, redistributable code sample; the skill should flag both.

### 8. Conference talks: regional Next calls are public, the Las Vegas one is not

Next Tokyo '26 published a call for breakout speakers (deadline 5 March 2026)
aimed at "engineers and business leaders working on the front lines" on
Google Cloud application development, data and AI. The Next Las Vegas site
shows dates (22 to 23 April 2026, next 13 to 15 April 2027) and no speaker
call; customer sessions there are arranged through account teams.
[Next Tokyo call](https://cloud.google.com/blog/ja/topics/next-tokyo/call-for-speakers-at-next-tokyo-26),
[Next Vegas](https://www.googlecloudevents.com/next-vegas), read 15 Oct 2026.

**Apply here:** the talk proposal template should fit a regional call (title,
abstract, audience, takeaways, speaker bio) and be sent to the account team for
the flagship event.

### 9. Anatomy of a Google Cloud agent story

FairPrice Group: headline "redefines retail experiences with AI agents"; a
"Google Cloud results" box of three bullets ("up to 18% click rate", "reduces
ad creation time by 10x while eliminating cost by up to 100x"); narrative
sections; a named quote with title; footer "Industry: Retail, Location:
Singapore, Products: ... Google ADK, Gemini, Model Context Protocol ...". The
text names "the Google Cloud Agent Development Kit (Google ADK)". LiveX AI:
results box of four bullets, one quantified ("response times by 6x with GKE
Autopilot"), plus "Why Google Cloud?" section. Color Health's press release
(16 Oct 2025) names Gemini 2.5, ADK and Agent Engine, carries CEO quotes from
both companies, and states projected impact only, with no measured outcome.
Google's "Customers are putting Gemini to work" post gives dollar and time
figures (Bell Canada "$20 million", Best Buy "up to 90 seconds faster", Elanco
"$1.9 million since launching last year") without baselines or methods.
[FairPrice](https://cloud.google.com/customers/fairpricegroup/),
[LiveX](https://cloud.google.com/customers/livex-ai),
[Color press release](https://www.googlecloudpresscorner.com/2025-10-16-Color-Health-and-Google-Cloud-Partner-to-Expand-Access-to-Breast-Cancer-Screening,-Augmenting-Clinical-Care-with-AI),
[Gemini at work](https://blog.google/products/google-cloud/gemini-at-work-ai-agents/),
read 15 Oct 2026.

**Apply here:** mirror the shape (results box, challenge, solution with named
products, quotes with titles, footer), but require each number to carry a
baseline, timeframe, sample and method in the draft, with a shorter public
version derived from it.

### 10. General case-study craft

HubSpot's guide prescribes title, subtitle, executive summary with two or three
metrics, about the company, challenges, solution, results, quotes, future plans
and a call to action; it recommends a release form covering names, logos,
titles and images, a 30 to 60 minute interview with questions sent in advance,
and "the more numbers, the better". That is marketing advice, not a measured
finding.
[HubSpot guide](https://blog.hubspot.com/blog/tabid/6307/bid/33282/the-ultimate-guide-to-creating-compelling-case-studies.aspx),
read 15 Oct 2026.

**Apply here:** generate the interview question list from the project's
evidence gaps, and produce a release-form checklist rather than legal text.

## What is documented versus assumed

Documented: terms section 9; approved and banned attribution phrases; logo
requests via the Google Cloud contact; trademark mechanics; Marketplace and
Agent Card requirements; ADK contribution paths and CLA; Next Tokyo call;
story structure on cloud.google.com. Not documented (ask the account team or
partner manager): how a story is selected and produced, timelines, what Google
asks the customer to supply, Partner Marketing Studio contents, Next Vegas
customer sessions, Agent Garden contribution, any "Built with" badge program
(none was found; badges referenced are partner and certification badges).

## Internal reuse

One evidence file feeds four outputs. Internal write-up: the full draft with
methods, raw numbers and confidential detail. Talk proposal: title, 150-word
abstract, audience level, three takeaways, demo plan, speaker bio. Blog or
Medium post: the solution section expanded with architecture and code. One-page
leave-behind: headline, results box, three-sentence challenge and solution,
one quote, diagram, contact. Each derived output inherits the approval status
of the sentences it reuses.

## Case-study draft template

```
Title: <Customer> <verb> <outcome> with AI agents built with Google Cloud
Subtitle: one sentence, challenge and benefit
Results box (3 to 4 bullets): metric, before -> after, timeframe
About <Customer>: 2 to 3 sentences, industry, size, location
Challenge: 2 to 3 paragraphs, the measured problem and its cost
Solution: what was built, ADK agents and tools, Gemini model, runtime
  (Agent Engine, Cloud Run or GKE), data sources, guardrails, evaluation
  Architecture diagram: components, trust boundaries, data flow
Results: each number with baseline, period, sample, method, owner
Quote(s): name, title, company; approved in writing
What's next: planned extensions, rollout, measurement plan
Footer: Industry, Location, Products (exact Google names)
Attribution line: "Built with Google Cloud" or "Built with Google Gemini"
```

Draft rules: say "Google Cloud" in full, never "GCP"; "Google Cloud Agent
Development Kit (ADK)" on first use; no "powered by"; no logos; no partnership
claims unless a partner agreement exists; mark every unapproved sentence.

## Submission checklist

1. Relationship: customer agreement type, Partner Advantage tier, startup
   program enrolment; name of the Google account manager or partner manager.
2. Internal approvals: customer executive sponsor, legal, PR or comms, security
   (architecture diagram and data flows), each dated.
3. Numbers: for each claim, baseline, measurement window, sample size, method,
   data owner, and whether it may be published, ranged or must be omitted.
4. Quotes: text, speaker, title, written approval, employee media clearance.
5. Diagram: reviewed for secrets, internal hostnames and customer data.
6. Timeline: project start, launch, measurement dates, publication target.
7. Attribution language chosen from the approved list; trademark lint passed.
8. Open asks for Google: logo use, joint announcement, story production,
   Next session, Marketplace interest; each marked "requires Google consent".
9. Derived assets: internal write-up, talk proposal, blog draft, one-pager,
   optional sanitised sample for `adk-samples/contrib` with CLA status.
10. Contacts: customer spokesperson, technical lead, marketing owner, Google
    contact, with the email thread or ticket where the pack was sent.
